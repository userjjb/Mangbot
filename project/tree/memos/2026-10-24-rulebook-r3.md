**How this was made.** The numbers come from scripts:
- A shared generation model, `objgen.py`:
  - every kind, with exact `get_obj_num` odds, the `kind_is_good` filter and the 1-in-20 level boost;
  - the `apply_magic` power roll;
  - one weapon or armour item's value at any power, cursed egos included;
  - the odds of normal and special artifacts.
- `build_drops.py`, which combines `monster_death` with that model:
  - the exact kind mix at each level;
  - Monte Carlo per kind for the plusses and egos;
  - closed forms for gold.

Two Clerks checked it:
- **D1** audited every mechanic line by line against the C code. It found no wrong rule. `m_bonus` uses a rounded Gaussian instead of the server's
  table `randnor`, which puts values 0–4% high.
- **D2** tested the model on Dive04's and dive03's logs. 444 of 445 gold piles fall inside the formula's range per metal, and the means match:
  copper 28.6 observed vs 28.5 predicted, silver 43.4 vs 43.5. The outlier is 3000 "gold" picked up in town, a grant rather than a drop. I re-checked the gold with my own script.

- **Drops per kill.** DROP_60 adds 0.6 and DROP_90 adds 0.9; DROP_1D2/2D2/3D2/4D2 add 1.5/3/4.5/6. Each drop is gold with 50% chance, unless the race is ONLY_ITEM or ONLY_GOLD.

- **Gold piles** are small: about 26 gold at level 0, 41 at level 10, 65 at level 20.
- **Artifact values** are kept out of `e_value`. They are rare and huge, so they are reported as P and expected cost instead.

## 2. Sold unidentified, a drop is worth much less [code ✓]

**Shops price on what the player knows.** `store.c:192` prices a sale with `object_value(p_ptr, o)`.
- **Unidentified items** go through `object_value_base` (`object2.c:1362-1395`, `1075-1090`):
  - weapons and armour sell at the **kind's base cost**, whatever their plusses or ego;
  - an unaware flavour gets a fixed 20 (potion or scroll), 50 (wand), 70 (staff), 90 (rod) or 45 (ring or amulet);
  - an item that felt cursed or broken sells for 0.
- **Once an item is identified**, `object_value_real` applies (and selling identifies it, but only after the price is set).

The tables' `e_value_unid` assumes aware flavours. Unidentified sales realise:
- **55–72% of true value for normal drops** (L5–L30);
- **17–29% for DROP_GOOD drops**;
- **4–14% for DROP_GREAT drops**.

So, for 2.2: **identify every {good}/{excellent} drop before selling it, and every DROP_GOOD unique's drop**. Your town kit already
identifies "unknowns worth more than a scroll". Make sure it compares the *true* expected value (`feelings.csv`), not the base cost the
shop would quote. A Bullroarer drop sold unidentified gives up about 3,200 of 3,900.

## 3. Value fix in `feelings.csv` (release 1–2 bug) [code ✓]

`object_value_real` adds 100 × (to-hit + to-dam + to-AC) **including the kind's own plusses** (`object2.c:1297,1311`). Ammo is the exception: it adds 5 × (to-hit + to-dam).
It also adds extra damage dice × sides × 100, and credits pval for every pval flag of the kind (e.g. a digger's tunnelling).
Releases 1–2 left out the kind's own plusses, the extra dice and the digger credit. Your `itemvalue.py` already counts displayed plusses, so the
two now agree. Changes at level 20, {good}:

| kind | r2 | r3 |
|---|---|---|
| dragon scale mails (+10) | 40,400 | 41,200 |
| Shield of Deflection (+10) | 10,398 | 11,399 |

| Rusty Chain Mail | 947 | 2 (usually still negative AC) |
| Broken Dagger/Sword | ~800 | ~230 |
| diggers | | +100–150 |

**Bows were missing from `feelings.csv`** (a parse filter). They are in now. `jewellery.csv` was rebuilt under Python 3.12 (r2 used the system
3.6, whose random draws differ). The differences are 6% or less, within the noise.

## 5. Your step-2.2 questions

1. **Restore Charisma.** Dive04's natural CHR is **4**: a Rot jelly drained it to 3 at 550 ft (`operations.md` m-notes). So Restore gives
   **3 → 4 only**. The 1.5–2.2× applies at CHR 18, which is only reachable with gain-Charisma potions, and town doesn't sell those.
   - **What 3 → 4 is worth.** In `shop_owners.csv`, every non-Black-Market owner pays 5 points more at CHR 4 (+13% on sales) and charges 5 points less.
     m17–m23 sold 5,339 gold, about 760 per mission (`data/scorecard/V1_value_events.csv`), so that's about 100 + 25 gold a mission.
   - **Cost.** About 470 at CHR 3 (base 300), so it pays back in about 4 missions, if nothing drains CHR again.
   - **Verdict.** Put it in the lowest tier, bought only with gold to spare after the kit. Keeping the Pilot from meleeing Rot jellies and other CHR drainers is worth more.
2. **Tier order.** I agree: WoR, then the supply minimums, then Restore STR/DEX/CON, then the kit's extras. STR and DEX set blows (`char_blows_table.csv`), and CON sets HP.
   The kit levels are the shops memo's, so no change.
3. **Devices never to sell** (for a warrior, at one of each):
   - **Escape and travel:** Staff of Teleportation, Rod of Recall, Wand of Teleport Other, Staff of Speed.
   - **Knowing the level:** Door/Stair Location, Detect Evil / Detection, Enlightenment and Magic Mapping.
   - **Perception**, which saves Identify scrolls.
   - **Wand of Stone to Mud**, for no-path emergencies like m17's at 650 ft.
   - **Healing and curing** staffs and rods.

   **Fine to sell:**
