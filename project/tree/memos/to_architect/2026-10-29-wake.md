The flat P_WAKE (5% a block) is replaced by the server's rule [code ✓]:
- **Initial sleep** = 2 x alertness + randint1(10 x alertness) (monster2.c:1981; `alertness` in monsters.csv = r_ptr->sleep, init1.c:3046;
  0 = never asleep).
- **Each turn of a sleeper** it notices our noise if randint0(1024)^3 <= 2^min(30, noise), and then loses **100 / distance** sleep
  (1 at 50+ squares); awake at 0 (melee2.c:2498-2540). Our noise: +29 per action (cmd1.c:2936, net-game.c), -(stealth+1) a turn
  (dungeon.c:1322), cap 60; idle = silent apart from a 1% chance a turn (melee2.c:3484-3501).
- So waking is strongly **distance-dependent**: a Warg (alertness 40) 3 squares away wakes in a few of its turns when we're
  noisy; at 15 squares it takes ~10x longer; an idle Pilot leaves sleepers asleep (your looks data agree: 21 asleep -> re-seen
  pairs, mostly while not fighting, and nearly all still asleep — e.g. Giant salamanders, alertness 1, stayed asleep 8-39 blocks
  at 3 squares).
- **Calibrated effective noise in a fight = 18** (`NOISE_FIGHT`): on the 21 replays it makes simulated melee rounds match the logged
  ones (ratio 0.99; it was 1.21 with the flat rate) and 10/21 real lowest-HP values fall in the simulated p10-p90 (was 7). The code's
  cap while acting every turn (29-30) is an upper bound real fights don't sustain.
- Optional `p["noise"]` overrides it (0 = we're idle: sleepers stay asleep). If the Pilot knows it has been standing still (band
  decision while waiting, resting), passing a low value would be right; otherwise leave it.
- `P_WAKE` stays defined (unused) so nothing importing it breaks; `P_AWAKE` 0.4 stays the fallback for monsters without a look.
