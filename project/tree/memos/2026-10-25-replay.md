# Memo: the estimator calibrated on 21 real emergencies: what it gets right, what it would change, and its budget (Advisor → Architect, 2026-10-25)

This is the replay study the user approved (`to_advisor/2026-10-25-estimator-go.md`). It follows `2026-10-25-estimator.md`. Audit trail: `Advisor/studies/2026-10-25-replay/`.

**How this was made:**
- **Replay files.** Clerks R1 and R2 turned the 21 episodes below 60% HP (m17-m32) into replay files (`replays/ep<idx>.json`). Each holds the state at fight start, every arrival, our actions with their effect times, the HP series, every melee and spell message, monster positions over time, and a 41×41 map window.
- **My calibration scripts**, all in `Advisor/data/estimator/`:
  - `replay.py` replays the logged actions;
  - `calib_blows.py` checks per blow;
  - `calib_adj.py` counts simultaneous attackers;
  - `calib_move.py` checks monster movement;
  - `replay_options.py` and `options_sim.choose` rank the options at each episode's start.
- **Clock:** one game turn is 0.625 s at factor 1 (`world.py` TICK_S), measured at 0.65 s, and about 3.3 s in the bubble (R2).

## 1. The model's parts, checked against the logs

| part | test | result | action |
|---|---|---|---|
| monster hit chance at our AC | 976 logged blows (hit/miss messages, blow i = message i of a round) | observed 0.572 vs model 0.518 | scaled ×1.10, now 0.570 |
| damage per hit (dice; HURT reduced by AC) and spells | predicted from the logged hits, vs real loss + heals, per episode | **median 1.12, IQR 0.85-1.27** | kept. The residual is expected: unseen "It" hits, poison and cuts aren't counted |
| attack rate (recoil: a round costs 2 actions) | round interval per single monster vs 2/speed | **median 1.04** | kept |
| **how many attack at once** | distinct attackers per 2 turns | **mean about 2, max 3**, with 5-8 open squares around us and 7-20 monsters in view | cap of 3 adjacent |
| **monsters closing in** | logged tracks vs "walk straight at us" | non-adjacent monsters close at **~0.4 of the model's rate** while we stand still. Many in view are asleep or haven't noticed us (monlist has no asleep flag) | each not-yet-engaged monster is awake with P = 0.4 |
| whole episodes, our logged actions forced | real lowest HP inside the simulated 10th-90th percentile | **7-10 of 21**. Simulated rounds are 1.2× the real count | residual bias: too many rounds |

**The atomic mechanics are right.** The remaining error is **engagement**: the model's monsters stay on us; real ones get stepped away from, flee, wander or sleep. One distance per monster can't capture this.

**The bias errs harsh,** toward escaping early. That is the safe direction for a decision aid, but it makes the model unsuitable for predicting exact HP.

## 2. What it would change, with the four rule fixes in the base policy

**Decision rule** (`options_sim.choose`): keep the rules unless an option is **clearly** better on the same random seeds. That means one of:
- the rules are unsafe (P(HP ≤ 10%) > 2%, or 10th-percentile lowest HP < 30%) and the option is safe;
- or both are safe, and the option's **XP net of gold** (1 XP per gold) beats the rules by more than 2 standard errors of the paired difference and by ≥ 10%.

Without this margin the top choice flipped with sample size in half the states. Most options are within noise of each other.

**Results over the 21 episode starts** (stable at both 40 and 100 rollouts per option):

| episode | rules | estimator | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 18 of 21 | — | **keeps the rules** | no option clearly better |
| m32 08:09 (Nar alone, 290 HP) | fight, then CCW below 50%: about **750 gold of cures** | **Speed first** | same safety, more XP, about 700 gold saved. The 10-25 Speed-first rule fires only with 2+ adjacent or when slowed |
| m30 06:43 (Druid, then hounds, Umber hulk) | borderline unsafe (P near death 0.03-0.04) | **a buff or cure at the start** (Berserk at n=40, CCW at n=100); both safe | the option differs with n; the verdict "rules marginal here" is stable |
| m22 02:05 (Boldor, yeeks, Druid; 32 monsters) | unsafe (0.15) | **recall + Phase** (least risky; nothing is safe) | the one truly bad state |

**Unstable** at the margin: m23 Phase, m27 Phase, m22 02:11 recall. These are within noise.

**What this means:**
1. **With the four fixes, the rules are right in most real emergencies**, and the estimator mostly confirms them.
2. **One more rule captures its largest robust gain:** Speed first also against **one strong monster** (a unique, or level ≥ clvl − 5) once HP is below 75% or a cure would otherwise be needed. That is the m32 case.
3. **The estimator's remaining value is in states the rules don't cover:** the rare crowd where every option is unsafe (choose the least risky), and marginal states like m30's start.

## 3. Budget (live server, no freeze)

| rollouts per option | median decision | max (crowds of 30+ including arrivals) |
|---|---|---|
| 40 | **0.39 s** | 1.28 s |
| 100 | 0.96 s | 3.1 s |

All runs used Python 3.12, one core, 10-12 options. With n = 40 the decision fits the 0.6 s turn outside the bubble in most states, and the 3 s turn inside it always.

**Recommendation:**
- run at the emergency trigger, with n = 40;
- drop options that can't apply (no stairs known, no staff, no Teleportation);
- in a crowd of 15+, cap the arrivals list to the nearest 15.

`evaluate_fast` (successive halving) exists, but `choose` with n = 40 is simpler and gave the same robust verdicts.

## 4. Recommendations

1. **Rule now:** Speed first against one strong monster (above). Unit test from m32 08:09: Nar adjacent, 290/313 HP, Speed in the pack.
2. **Estimator: shadow mode first.** Log `choose()`'s verdict and its `why` beside each emergency decision, without acting, for 3-5 missions. Act on it only where it disagrees with the rules *and* the reason is "rules unsafe".
   - Copy `options_sim.py` (`choose`, `rollout`, `base_policy`) and `replay.py` into `tools/`, as you prefer.
   - The base policy must mirror the Pilot's rules. When the Pilot's rules change, update `base_policy`, or the comparison is against the wrong baseline.
3. **The data that would let the model go further:**
   - **an asleep flag per monster.** It is the biggest single error source: the 0.4 awake factor stands in for it;
   - **the 21×21 map window in emergency records** (you've added it from today; thanks). With it, the next version can place monsters on the grid, walk them straight at us around walls (no flow), and model our own steps and Phase landings. That should fix the engagement bias;
   - **our melee as logged commands** (R2: blows have no ack), so a replay can force exactly when we attacked.
4. **Set-pieces:** a Nar-alone case (does Speed-first save cures?) and an m22-like crowd with nothing safe (does the estimator pick the least risky option?).

## Contradictions and corrections

| belief | finding |
|---|---|
| my 10-25 memo: "the m30 replay is too mild" | the first prototype was too mild. Once calibrated (summons, statuses, cap 3, awake 0.4) it runs slightly **harsh** (simulated rounds 1.2× real) |
| `time_factor` records as the game clock | they can carry a stale measured rate (9× from a rest) into a fight (ep38), so the replay uses 0.2 in the bubble (m25+), else 1 |
| E1 episode "start" = fight start | E1's start is its trigger. m30's fight began 8 s earlier, with an unseen Druid (R2) |

## What I lacked

- **No asleep flag.** This is the main calibration gap.
- **No grid positions with walls** for episodes before today's 21×21 windows.
- **Our melee isn't logged as commands.**
- **Before m24 there are no inventory snapshots** (R1 reconstructed them from messages), and **before m25 there was no bubble**, so these episodes aren't directly comparable with later ones.
