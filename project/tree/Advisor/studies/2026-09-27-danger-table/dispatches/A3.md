# Dispatch: how out-of-depth (OOD) monsters appear in MAngband 1.5

- Brief: A3 — monster level selection (get_mon_num boost), vaults/pits/nests, uniques, summons,
  wilderness, shared/regenerated levels, and player-visible signs of OOD danger.
- Clerk run: 2026-09-27; sources covered: github/src/server/generate.c, monster2.c, dungeon.c,
  wilderness.c, cave.c (is_quest, spot-check), lib/edit/vault.txt, lib/edit/monster.txt (spot-check),
  src/server/mdefines.h, src/common/defines.h.

## Answer

Out-of-depth monsters reach a player through five independent mechanisms, each with an exact
level formula: (1) a "nasty monster" boost baked into every `get_mon_num()` call (1/50 chance,
twice, each adding up to +5 effective levels); (2) vault/pit/nest room generation, which always
requests monsters several to forty levels deeper than the room's actual dungeon depth; (3) the
`&`/`@`/`9`/`8`/`,` symbols hard-coded into vault layouts (`vault.txt`), up to Depth+40; (4)
`summon_specific()`, which averages the summoner's level with the dungeon depth and adds 5; and
(5) wilderness terrain/distance formulas. Uniques are not blocked from being deep or shallow by
depth at all (only `FORCE_DEPTH` monsters are), and are tracked per-player, not per-server, so a
unique can respawn for a player who has not yet killed it even after another player has. Levels
are per-depth, shared while any player remains, and freshly regenerated only once every player
leaves — so naive solo stair-scumming does get a new level, but the "feeling" readout is
suppressed if you do it faster than 1000 game turns.

## Findings

1. **NASTY_MON boost in `get_mon_num()`**: for any `level > 0`, up to two independent rolls of
   `randint0(NASTY_MON)==0` (NASTY_MON=50, 1/50 each) each add `min(level/4+2, 5)` to the
   *selection* level before the monster table is filtered. Max total boost from both rolls is 10
   (both procs is 1/2500). [SEEN] (monster2.c:559-580; NASTY_MON defined mdefines.h:179 "1/chance
   of inflated monster level"). Deterministic increment, not literally a dice roll as the brief's
   "+d(?)" guessed.
2. After picking a candidate monster, `get_mon_num()` re-rolls the whole table up to twice more
   (60% once, 10% twice) and **keeps whichever candidate has the larger `abs(level)`** — doesn't
   raise the depth ceiling but skews toward the hardest monster the (possibly boosted) level
   allows. [SEEN] (monster2.c:666-713).
3. **Vault "nook" monsters** (`vault_monsters()`, used inside large/special rooms, e.g. type-4
   rooms) always request `monster_level = Depth + 2`. [SEEN] (generate.c:1076-1099, e.g. call at
   generate.c:1315). Room type 4 min level is 0, so this can appear from DL1 (generate.c:265).
4. **Monster nests** (build_type5) request `get_mon_num(Depth+10)` (64 draws), themed by
   `tmp = randint1(Depth)`: jelly if tmp<30, animal if tmp<50, else undead — jelly nests dominate
   until DL30, animal DL30+, undead DL50+, matching the code's own comment. [SEEN]
   (generate.c:1889-1934, 1842-1851). Room min level = 5 (generate.c:266).
5. **Monster pits** (build_type6) request `get_mon_num(Depth+10)` (16 draws, sorted, every other
   kept), themed by the same `randint1(Depth)` roll: orc<20, troll<40, giant<60, dragon<80, else
   demon. [SEEN] (generate.c:2024-2201). Room min level = 5 (generate.c:267).
6. **Vault symbols** in `build_vault()` (shared by lesser (type7) and greater (type8) vaults):
   `&`→Depth+5, `@`→Depth+11, `9`→Depth+9 monster/Depth+7 treasure, `8`→**Depth+40**
   monster/Depth+20 treasure, `,`→50% chance Depth+3 monster and independently 50% chance Depth+7
   treasure. [SEEN] (generate.c:2436-2483). `lib/edit/vault.txt`'s own symbol legend gives the
   identical numbers (vault.txt:29-33), so code and data agree.
7. **Room-type gating**: `room[]` table minimum depths: types 1-4 = DL0, type 5 (nest) = DL5,
   type 6 (pit) = DL5, type 7 (lesser vault) = DL5, type 8 (greater vault) = DL10. [SEEN]
   (generate.c:259-270, `room_build()` check at generate.c:3054).
8. **Why vaults/pits/nests still feel rare at low depth**: reaching the "unusual room" branch
   needs **two independent** `randint0(DUN_UNUSUAL) < Depth` checks (DUN_UNUSUAL=200) — probability
   ≈(Depth/200)² per room slot, before the 10/15/15/10% split between greater vault/lesser
   vault/pit/nest. [SEEN] (generate.c:3213-3243; constant generate.c:109). Roughly: ≈0.03%/slot at
   DL10, ≈2.3%/slot at DL30, ≈6.3%/slot at DL50 for a greater-vault attempt specifically.
   [INFERRED from those two facts, not simulated.]
9. **Lesser vaults can carry the worst symbol too.** Parsing all 149 entries in `vault.txt`: 75
   greater (typ 8), 67 lesser (typ 7). 60/75 greater vaults contain `8`; **4/67 lesser vaults also
   contain `8`** ("The Shaft", "Backdoor Surprise", "Ancient Cave", "Zoo of Concentrated Death").
   [SEEN] (vault.txt:3053 ff.). Since lesser vaults need only DL5, a Depth+40 monster/Depth+20
   object can in principle be requested as early as DL5. In "The Shaft" the `8` monsters sit
   behind permanent rock (`X`), reachable only through doors off a trapped corridor — a
   deliberately caged trap, not an open room. [SEEN] (vault.txt:3053-3065).
10. **Uniques are not depth-restricted by default.** `place_monster_one()` only blocks a unique for
    `allow_unique_level()` failing, `cur_num>=max_num`, or `FORCE_DEPTH` (if the unique carries
    it). No `r_ptr->level` vs `Depth` check blocks a unique from appearing shallower than its
    native level; being deeper only raises the level "rating" (2x delta-depth for uniques, 1x for
    normal monsters). [SEEN] (monster2.c:1833-1872).
11. **`allow_unique_level()` is per-player, not per-server**: a unique may generate on a depth if
    *at least one* player there has `r_killed[r_idx]==FALSE`, regardless of other players' kill
    records. [SEEN] (monster2.c:17-35). MAngband-specific extension of vanilla's global unique
    tracking; explains why a shallow unique can keep reappearing for players who haven't beaten it.
12. **Grip and Fang** are level 2 (`W:2:1:0:30`), confirming they roam shallow. [SEEN]
    (monster.txt:585-608). **Hounds** (`Z`) carry `F:FRIENDS` (group placement via
    `place_monster_group()`), e.g. Light/Dark hound at level 15. [SEEN] (monster.txt:2389-2411;
    monster2.c:2003, 2155-2158).
13. **`summon_specific()` formula**: picked level = `get_mon_num((Depth+lev)/2+5)`, `lev` = the
    summoner's own level. [SEEN] (monster2.c:2552-2591). Differs from the brief's guess of "+1":
    the real constant is **+5**, so summons skew harder than a naive average.
14. **Per-turn dungeon spawn**: each player, once per 50 game turns, rolls
    `randint0(MAX_M_ALLOC_CHANCE)==0` (=160, 1/160) to add a monster via `alloc_monster` at
    `monster_level = Depth` (still subject to finding 1's boost). [SEEN] (dungeon.c:490-628;
    mdefines.h:204). Each player rolls independently, so a busier shared level spawns monsters
    proportionally faster. [INFERRED.]
15. **Wilderness surface monsters** scale with `radius` (distance from town) and terrain, set in
    `init_terrain()`: grassland/lake `1+r/2`; forest `5+r/2`; swamp `12+r/2` (x2 at night); dense
    forest `15+r/2` (+10 at night); wasteland `20+r/2`. [SEEN] (wilderness.c:1548-1637). The
    separate per-turn spawn (`wild_add_monster`, same 1/160-per-50-turns cadence) instead uses a
    flat `2+r/2` regardless of terrain. [SEEN] (dungeon.c:619-627, wilderness.c:356-393).
    Dwellings can host a "home owner" at level = full `radius` (no halving; 75% inhabited x 80%
    "at home"), or "invaders" at `radius/2+1` (~50% if uninhabited). [SEEN] (wilderness.c:967-1001).
16. **Shared/regenerated levels**: `players_on_depth[Depth]` tracks arrivals/departures
    (dungeon.c:1695-1722); at 0 (non-town, non-special depth) the level is deallocated
    (dungeon.c:2035-2046); a player then entering that depth triggers a brand-new
    `generate_cave()` and a fresh feeling (dungeon.c:2068-2081). So solo stair-scumming does get a
    fresh level, but the cave persists and is genuinely shared while any other player remains.
    [SEEN]
17. **Level feeling** (`do_cmd_feeling`, cmd4.c:164-211): one 0–10 scale whose text reads like
    vanilla's *loot-quality* feeling ("superb", "excellent", "good"), not a danger phrase. It comes
    from a `rating` accumulator that vaults/pits/nests each raise by +10 (plus `v_ptr->rat` for
    vaults), and that any deeper-than-room monster raises by `(level-Depth)`, doubled for uniques.
    [SEEN] (generate.c:1957, 2268, 2516; monster2.c:1850-1871; thresholds generate.c:4226-4252).
    So it warns of OOD danger indirectly, but wording can't distinguish loot from monster risk. A
    level generated <1000 game turns ago has its feeling forced to 0 and auto-scum disabled for
    that generation — a soft brake on fast feeling-scumming. [SEEN] (generate.c:4241-4249).
18. **Recognisable OOD signs in play** (no dedicated "danger UI" found): permanent rock (`X`)
    walling a room interior is a vault tell (generate.c:2385-2388); monster recall (the race's
    level, via monster lore) is the only reliable per-monster depth signal; comments note very
    dangerous monsters seen in line-of-sight are deliberately placed asleep ("a sporting chance")
    (monster2.c:1786-1791).

## Contradictions and surprises

1. Brief's "already known": greater vaults rare below ~DL30. Code's *hard* gate is only DL10
   (generate.c:269); rarity instead comes from the two-stage `(Depth/200)²` gate before the
   greater-vault branch is even rolled — roughly 1%/level at DL10 rising to ~11% at DL30 and ~31%
   at DL50 by my calculation (INFERRED, not simulated). So the claim's *degree* holds but the
   mechanism is a smooth curve, not a floor at DL30.
2. Surprise: **lesser vaults** (DL5 minimum) can carry the deadliest symbol `8` (Depth+40 monster
   / Depth+20 treasure), same as greater vaults — 4 of 67 do (e.g. "The Shaft", "Backdoor
   Surprise", vault.txt:3053-3082). In "The Shaft" these are sealed behind permanent rock, i.e. a
   deliberate trap, so real risk depends on the player breaking in.
3. The unique `cur_num>=max_num` check inside `get_mon_num()` is only a commented-out block
   (monster2.c:621-626); real enforcement is later, in `place_monster_one()`
   (monster2.c:1833-1839). Functionally harmless (redundant, not missing) but worth flagging.
4. `allow_unique_level()` (monster2.c:17-35) is per-player unique tracking, not in the brief's
   "already known" list — explains why a shallow unique can keep reappearing for players who
   haven't beaten it, even on a shared level.
5. Brief guessed the summon formula as "+1"; the actual 1.5 constant is **+5** (monster2.c:2574).
6. The 1.5 level-feeling text (cmd4.c:164-177) reads as loot-quality, not danger-specific, even
   though the underlying "rating" is substantially driven by OOD monsters (2x weight for uniques)
   and vault/pit/nest presence — the wording can't tell the player which is which.

## Leads

- `check_special_level()`/`special_levels[]` (dungeon.c:2565-2573, MAX_SPECIAL_LEVELS=10,
  defines.h:171) and `is_quest()`/`is_quest_level()` (cave.c:4222,4242) govern hand-built static
  levels; their monster-placement code was not read (out of assigned sources) — needed if the
  danger table must know whether fixed low-DL static levels can carry very-OOD monsters.
- `FORCE_DEPTH` monster list not enumerated; needs a `monster.txt` scan (`F:FORCE_DEPTH`) to know
  which named races are "floor-only" and thus exempt from shallow-unique-style roaming.
- `MAX_SIGHT` (in `alloc_monster(Depth, MAX_SIGHT+5, FALSE)`, dungeon.c:626) sets minimum spawn
  distance from the player; exact value and any stealth/detection interaction not verified.
- Whether `object_level` boosts in vault symbols (Depth+7/+20/+40) matter for OOD monster drops
  specifically was not checked — out of scope per the brief ("skip object generation").
