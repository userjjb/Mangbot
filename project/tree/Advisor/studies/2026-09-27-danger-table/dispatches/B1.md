# Dispatch: B1 — danger table, dungeon levels 0–10 (159 monsters, 0–500 ft)
- Brief: rate every monster in `scratch/B1_input.csv` for danger/response/neutraliser at the depth of
  its own native level (character state: clvl 1–15, HP 10–150, save 16–30%, AC ~20–30, no resists).
- Clerk run: 2026-09-27; sources covered: `scratch/B1_input.csv` (159 rows, read in full),
  `dispatches/A1.md` (legend, read in full), 18 raw spot-checks of `github/lib/edit/monster.txt`
  against the CSV (all matched exactly), `src/server/melee1.c:90-127` (`check_hit`) read directly to
  resolve a discrepancy in the paralysis/gaze mechanic.

## Answer
159/159 rated; output in `scratch/B1_table.csv`. Distribution: 16×trivial(0), 46×easy(1), 52×care(2),
37×dangerous(3), 8×lethal(4), 0×flee(5). The dominant danger driver is **FRIENDS/ESCORT group spawns**
(15 of 45 monsters rated 3+), followed by **damaging/status spellcasters** (~11), **breath weapons**
(8, all at dl8–10), and **uniques as a class** (9 of the 11 uniques in this band rate 3+). No monster
in 0–500 ft needs a flat "flee on sight" (5) rating; the worst are 8 lethal (4) uniques/monsters best
handled with AVOID/LEAVE. One mechanic (`GAZE:PARALYZE`) turned out to work very differently from the
brief's "already known" folklore — see Contradictions.

## Findings
### Ranking of danger features (counts among the 45 rows rated 3 or 4)
1. **FRIENDS/ESCORT group spawns — 15 rows.** Jackal, Cave spider, Wood spider, Wolf (animal packs);
   Novice mage/priest/warrior (dl6 FRIENDS variants), Novice ranger/paladin (dl8 FRIENDS variants),
   Cave orc, Hill orc (orc packs); plus 4 uniques that bring an ESCORT/ESCORTS: Mughash, Lagduf,
   Grishnakh, Orfax. Per-monster damage is often modest, but simultaneous attackers in an open room can
   add up fast on a 10–150 HP character; the fix in every case is the same: fight in a corridor.
2. **Damaging/status spellcasters — ~11 rows.** Novice priest/paladin (`CAUSE_1` 3d8, avg 13.5, and the
   character's 16–30% save means it lands *most* of the time), Novice ranger (`MISSILE` 2d6+lvl/3),
   Novice mage/Dark elven mage (`BLIND`/`CONF`/`MISSILE`/`BA_POIS`), Orc shaman, Kobold shaman, Orfax,
   Pseudo-dragon, Tengu. `CAUSE_1` alone is the single biggest recurring "surprise": at 3d8 it can
   remove roughly a third to half of a fresh dl2–dl4 character's HP in one unsaved cast, and the
   character's save (16–30%) does not block it often.
3. **Breath weapons — 8 rows, all dl8–10.** Giant salamander (`BR_FIRE`) and the 7 baby/pseudo dragons
   (`BR_ELEC/COLD/POIS/ACID/FIRE/CONF/SOUN`). Elemental breaths (elec/cold/pois/acid/fire) = current
   HP/3, average ≈33 damage for the 100-HP baby dragons, against a dl9–10 character's assumed 100–150
   HP — a serious single hit, and per A1 breaths are never AC-reduced. The two non-elemental breaths
   (bronze `BR_CONF`, gold `BR_SOUN`) do only ≈17 (HP/6) but add a confusion or stun lock, which the
   danger scale treats as equally dangerous.
4. **Uniques as a class — 9 of 11 rate 3+.** Only Farmer Maggot (0, see below) and Smeagol (2, a fast
   invisible thief with negligible combat output) are low; Grip/Fang/Bullroarer rate 3 (speed/melee),
   and Mughash/Wormtongue/Lagduf/Brodda/Orfax/Grishnakh rate 4.
5. **`TELE_TO` (escape-denial) — 2 rows, small in count but mechanically distinct.** Tengu (1-in-3,
   33% per turn!) and Orfax (1-in-4). Both can drag the character back to them, which defeats the
   scale's usual AVOID response ("walk away") — noted specifically in their `notes`.

### Monsters rated 4 (lethal), one line each
- **Brodda, the Easterling** (dl9): 4×`HIT:HURT 1d12`, melee_max 48 — the single highest sustained
  melee output of any monster in this band (avg ≈26/turn before AC), no spells or escort needed.
- **Grishnakh, the Hill Orc** (dl10): 4 blows up to 1d12, melee_max 44, plus an ESCORT of orcs.
- **Lagduf, the Snaga** (dl8): 4 blows, melee_max 38, plus an ESCORT.
- **Mughash the Kobold Lord** (dl7): 3×`HIT:HURT 1d10` (melee_max 30) plus ESCORT+ESCORTS (brings a
  full kobold group) — matches the brief's "already known" list.
- **Wormtongue, Agent of Saruman** (dl8): `BO_COLD` (avg 23, unresisted), `BA_POIS` (avg 13,
  unresisted), `HEAL` (prolongs the fight), `SLOW` (only Free Action stops it) — matches "already
  known".
- **Orfax, Son of Boldor** (dl10): the only monster in this band with a summon spell (`S_MONSTER`,
  1-in-4 = 25% cast chance), plus `TELE_TO` (defeats simple avoidance), `SLOW`, `CONF`, `HEAL`, and an
  ESCORT+ESCORTS of yeeks — matches "already known" but is arguably nastier than its melee stats alone
  (17 melee_max) suggest.
- **Dark elven mage** (dl10, not a unique): 1-in-5 (20%) cast chance of `BA_POIS` (avg 13, unresisted)
  or `MISSILE` (avg 10) plus `BLIND`/`CONF` locks — the single most dangerous *non-unique* monster in
  the whole band; see "surprised me" below.
- **Pseudo-dragon** (dl10, not a unique): `BR_LITE`/`BR_DARK` (avg 33, plus blind), `CONF`, `SCARE`
  spells, HP 200 (very tanky, prolonging exposure) — more dangerous than the same-depth baby dragons.

### Paralyzers, blinders, confusers
- **Paralyze:** only Floating eye — see Contradictions, it deals **zero** direct damage.
- **Blind (real proc):** Bloodshot eye (`GAZE:BLIND 2d6`, dl7, power=2 with level=7 gives a real
  to-hit, unlike the floating eye), King cobra (`SPIT:BLIND`), Novice mage/Dark elven mage (`BLIND`
  spell), Pseudo-dragon (blind as a breath side-effect).
- **Confuse:** Grey mushroom patch, Brown mold (blow effect, low-tier); Novice mage, Dark elf, Dark
  elven mage, Orfax, Kobold shaman (spell); Baby bronze dragon (`BR_CONF` breath).
- **Summoners:** exactly one — **Orfax** (`S_MONSTER`, single summon, 25% cast chance). No other
  monster in the band carries any `S_` flag.

### Where the stat block surprised me
1. **Floating eye deals zero direct damage** (`B:GAZE:PARALYZE` with no dice, `melee_max=0`), and its
   to-hit is mostly the flat 5% "always hit" floor, not the power/level formula (see Contradictions) —
   the opposite of "floating eye = instant death."
2. **Farmer Maggot (unique, 350 HP) cannot hurt the player at all** — both blows are `MOAN` with no
   effect, 0 damage (`monster.txt:145-146`). A "unique" that is pure flavor text in combat.
3. **Green jelly (HP 176) and Red jelly (HP 208)** deal only 1d2/1d5 touch damage — huge HP walls that
   are a time sink, not a threat, though Red jelly's sheer HP means *many* STR-drain procs accumulate
   over the long grind, which is why it alone among the "eye/jelly, low power" family got bumped to 3.
4. **Dark elven mage vastly outclasses the plain Dark elf** of the same family: 20% cast frequency (vs
   10%), adds `BA_POIS` and `MISSILE` on top of `BLIND`/`CONF`/`DARKNESS`, and is immune to poison
   itself. It is the single most dangerous non-unique monster in the whole 0–500 ft band.
5. **Kobold shaman (dl3, not special-named)** has a 20% cast chance of `CAUSE_1` (avg 13.5, unsaved
   ~75–85% of the time at this depth) — a plain kobold variant that can nearly kill a fresh clvl3
   diver (assumed HP 30–60) in one lucky cast, disproportionate to its unremarkable appearance.
6. **Creeping copper/silver/gold coins are not stationary** — no `NEVER_MOVE` flag, and they carry
   `BASH_DOOR`, so they can chase, contrary to the classic "ambush-only" mimic assumption.

## Contradictions and surprises
1. **Floating eye's paralysis is far rarer than the brief's "already known" folklore implies, and it
   is not itself lethal.** `check_hit()` (`melee1.c:106-127`) computes `i = power + level*3`; for
   `GAZE:PARALYZE` (power 2, A1's blow-effect table) at the eye's native level 1, `i = 5`. Since a hit
   requires `randint1(i) > ac*3/4`, and player AC at dl1 is already ~10-20+ (threshold 7.5-15+), the
   formula term can essentially never fire — the only way the eye ever lands its gaze is via
   `check_hit`'s flat "always hit 5% of the time" floor (`k < 5`, out of the `k < 10` branch). And the
   blow itself carries **no damage dice** (`monster.txt:392`, `B:GAZE:PARALYZE`, blank dice field;
   `melee_max=0` in the parsed CSV) — so a lone floating eye cannot kill the character by itself even if
   the paralysis lands; it only leaves them helpless for a few turns. This is a genuine, evidence-backed
   contradiction of "floating eyes paralyse with their gaze if you melee them" being a serious solo
   threat — it's real but weak and non-lethal alone, dangerous only in combination with something else
   nearby (roaming monsters, other party members' fights). I rated it 2 (care), not 3, and flagged the
   nuance in its `notes`/`threats` fields. [SEEN]
2. **"Novice priests/mages summon" is not supported by this data.** Novice priest's spell list is
   `HEAL|SCARE|CAUSE_1` (no summon, no blink); Novice mage's is `BLINK|BLIND|CONF|MISSILE` (confirms
   blink, but no summon spell either). Across the entire 159-row input, the *only* monster with any
   `S_` (summon) flag is Orfax (`S_MONSTER`). If the brief's "already known" note is drawing on a
   different game version or a different encounter (e.g. a priest with `S_MONSTER` deeper in the game),
   it doesn't apply to any dl0-10 priest/mage variant in this server's monster.txt. [SEEN,
   `monster.txt:487-512,1071-1097`]
3. Other "already known" claims check out cleanly: Grip and Fang are confirmed fast (speed +10) but
   weak/fragile early killers (melee_max 4, HP 25) [SEEN]; Bullroarer, Mughash, Grishnakh are confirmed
   dangerous early uniques (rated 3/4/4) [SEEN]; Wormtongue and Orfax are confirmed dangerous uniques,
   and Orfax specifically is worse than his melee stats suggest once you add `TELE_TO`+summon [SEEN];
   floating eyes do paralyse via melee — confirmed mechanically, but weaker/rarer/non-lethal-alone than
   implied (see #1); worm masses and lice do breed (`MULTIPLY`), confirmed for 13 monsters, though the
   engine's 100-per-level cap (per A1) means the swarm builds slowly, not instantly; "Mean-looking
   mercenary matters only to brand-new characters" is confirmed almost literally — its 1d10 hit (max
   10) is close to a clvl1 character's ~10 HP. [SEEN]

## Leads
- `ARROW_1` (Novice ranger, dl4/dl8) and `ARROW_2` (Kobold archer) spell/attack damage formulas are not
  in the A1 legend (only breaths/bolts/balls are tabulated there); I treated them qualitatively as
  "ranged physical, adds to melee" without a number. Worth a follow-up grep of `melee2.c` for
  `RSF_ARROW_1`/`RSF_ARROW_2` if precise numbers matter for the danger list.
- `do_dec_stat()` exact drain amounts (used by every `LOSE_STR/DEX/CON/WIS/CHR` blow in this band —
  ~10 monsters) weren't traced by A1 past "damage + stat drain via do_dec_stat(); sustain-stat blocks
  it"; I treated all of these as "care/dangerous, permanent-ish stat loss, restorable by potion" without
  quantifying how many points per hit.
- `apply_nexus()` and `RES_DISE`/disenchant mechanics weren't traced in detail by A1 either; relevant to
  Disenchanter eye/mold (rated 2 here on the assumption disenchantment is annoying, not HP-threatening).
- Whether Grip and Fang ever co-spawn together (doubling their combined threat) wasn't checked; that's
  a spawn-placement question outside monster.txt/A1 scope.
