# Dispatch: Vaults from vault.txt and generate.c
- Brief: how vaults are built, symbol meanings, depths and odds, a parsed list of every vault (CSV), how to spot one from outside, danger at 250-1000 ft, and MAngband vault-like oddities.
- Clerk run: 2026-10-07; sources: github/src/server/generate.c (build_vault, build_type7/8, room_build, cave_gen, build_tunnel), init1.c (vault parser), mdefines.h, monster2.c (place_monster, get_mon_num), lib/edit/vault.txt (all 149 entries, parsed), lib/edit/terrain.txt, cave.c (map glyph path), wilderness.c (arena, moat).
- CSV: /projectnb/jbrcs/mangband/Advisor/studies/2026-10-07-mission15/scratch/V2_vaults.csv. Scripts: V2_parse.py and V2_stats.py in the same dir.

## Answer
1. A vault is a stamped template. `build_vault` lays floor on every non-space cell, then applies symbols. The outer wall `%` is ordinary granite, not permanent. Only `X` is permanent, and it is always inside the vault. So "permanent outer wall" is not how vaults look.
2. Lesser vaults (type 7) can appear from dungeon level 5 (250 ft). Greater vaults (type 8) can appear from level 10 (500 ft). Odds are low: about 1-3% per level at 250-600 ft for at least one lesser vault, about 1-2% for a greater vault at 500-600 ft, and about 5-7% lesser / 5% greater at 1000 ft. These are upper bounds.
3. Permanent walls and granite walls are drawn identically as white `#` by the server's terrain table. A recogniser can only use shape, not glyph.
4. The real danger in lesser vaults is the `8` symbol. It places a monster at depth +40 and an object at depth +20, and 4 of the 67 lesser vaults contain it. Most lesser vaults are mild: `&` is +5, `9` is +9, `@` is +11.

## Findings
1. Symbol meanings in `build_vault` [SEEN] (generate.c:2332-2486; vault.txt:21-32 legend matches the code):
   - `%` becomes FEAT_WALL_OUTER, which is granite (terrain.txt N:58). It is not permanent. Corridors may pierce it.
   - `#` becomes FEAT_WALL_INNER, which is granite.
   - `X` becomes FEAT_PERM_INNER (0x3D), which is permanent.
   - `+` is a secret door (`place_secret_door`).
   - `^` is a trap (`place_trap`).
   - `*` is 75% object, 25% trap, through `place_object(..., FALSE, FALSE)`.
   - `.` is plain floor. A space is a non-grid and is skipped.
   - Every non-space cell gets FEAT_FLOOR first, plus flags CAVE_ROOM|CAVE_ICKY (2369-2370).
   - `build_vault` never sets CAVE_GLOW, so vault interiors are unlit. [INFERRED from the code; I saw no glow set anywhere in it.]
2. Monster symbols, second pass (generate.c:2411-2481). All use `place_monster(Depth, y, x, slp=TRUE, grp=TRUE)`, so monsters start asleep and groups are allowed.
   - `&`: monster_level = Depth+5.
   - `@`: Depth+11.
   - `9`: monster Depth+9 plus a good object (`place_object(...TRUE,FALSE)`) at object level Depth+7.
   - `8`: monster Depth+40 plus a good and great object at object level Depth+20.
   - `,`: independently 50% a monster at Depth+3 and 50% an object at Depth+7.
   - Here `Depth` is the dungeon level (level 5 = 250 ft).
   - `get_mon_num` can add a further "nasty" boost on top: 1/50 chance per roll of +min(level/4+2, 5), rolled twice (monster2.c:559-575; I read the first roll and the start of the second). [SEEN]
   - The first-pass `^`/`+` cells and the `*` cells are placed before monsters.
3. Vault choice and sizes [SEEN].
   - `build_type7` and `build_type8` pick uniformly among vault.txt entries with typ 7 (or 8), by rejection sampling (2498-2506, 2538-2546). There is no depth weighting. A level-5 character can get the same lesser vault as a level-20 one.
   - Each adds the vault's rating to the level `rating`, and sets `good_item_flag` (feeling 1) when Depth<=50.
   - The init1.c parser caps lesser at 33x22 and greater at 66x44. Type 9 ("gigantic", up to 99x66) is accepted and converted to type 8 (init1.c:1005-1015).
   - `build_vault` aborts silently if the vault would touch the dungeon edge (2342-2346).
4. Level gates and odds [SEEN code, odds INFERRED].
   - `room[]` (generate.c:259-269): lesser needs Depth>=5 and a 3x2 block footprint (33x22). Greater needs Depth>=10 and a 6x4 footprint (66x44).
   - `cave_gen` tries 50 rooms (DUN_ROOMS=50, line 108). Each attempt rolls `randint0(200)<Depth` twice (lines 3214 and 3220), so p = (Depth/200)^2.
   - Then k=randint0(100): k<10 tries a greater vault, k<25 tries a lesser one (a failed greater falls through to lesser), and k<40 is a pit.
   - Per-level chance of at least one attempt, ignoring failed placements (overlap, edges, "crowded"). The same list is in V2_stats.py output.

     | Depth | Lesser | Greater |
     |---|---|---|
     | 5 (250 ft) | 0.8% | 0 |
     | 7 (350 ft) | 1.5% | 0 |
     | 9 (450 ft) | 2.5% | 0 |
     | 10 (500 ft) | 1.9% | 1.2% |
     | 12 (600 ft) | 2.7% | 1.8% |
     | 15 (750 ft) | 4.1% | 2.8% |
     | 20 (1000 ft) | 7.2% | 4.9% |

   - There is a small step down for lesser at 10 because the 10% greater share is taken from the lesser share. Destroyed levels (Depth>10, 1/15) build only trivial rooms (3159-3161, 3197-3203).
5. Vault.txt contents [SEEN, parsed with V2_parse.py]. There are 149 entries: 67 type 7, 75 type 8, 7 type 9 (counted as greater, 82 greater in total).
   - Seven entries named "Lesser vault (...)" are actually type 8: prison, camp, x-factor, nethack tiny castle, amenhotep I, osiris halls, Pattern. Two entries have row/column counts that do not match their D lines: "Lesser vault (prison)" and "nethack spiral".
   - Of the 67 lesser vaults, 31 have rating 5, 16 have rating 10, and the maximum is 50 (Zoo). They range from 5x5 to 22x22; the footprint area is 25-580 cells.
   - Lesser vaults with `8`: "The Shaft" (8), "Backdoor Surprise" (12), "Zoo of Concentrated Death" (25, rating 50, 15x15) and "Ancient Cave" (1). So 4/67 = 6% of lesser draws are Depth+40 monsters, which at levels 5-12 means monster level 45-52.
   - Lesser vaults with `@` (+11): 11 vaults, 1-4 each.
   - `9` (+9 and a good item): 27 vaults, 1-5 each.
   - Typical lesser vault is mostly `,` (50% monster at +3) plus a few `&`.
   - Secret doors: 57 of 67 lesser vaults have `+`. The 10 without any: Planet X, Turnabout, Camouflaged, Rock-Hard, Miniature Cell, mine, maze, Ancient Cave, Trap Vault, Trap Vault 2. How those are entered is not determined (INFERRED: by piercing `%` or tunnelling).
6. Outer walls [SEEN]. The outline of every vault in vault.txt is `%` (outer granite), with a few exceptions.
   - "Turnabout" and "Central" use `#` for their outline (inner granite).
   - "Brain's lair" has one `#` on the outline.
   - "nethack castle (almost)" has 2 `X` on the outline.
   - 12 lesser vaults have `X` inside: Planet X (56), Camouflaged (43), Workaround (117), bank (64), mine (64), maze (198), tower (43), Shaft (43), Zoo (4), Ancient Cave (86), Trap Vault (46), Trap Vault 2 (47). Greater vaults nearly all have 100-1500 `X`.
   - So of lesser vaults, 55 of 67 are pure granite throughout. They can be tunnelled into.
7. Corridors reach vaults [SEEN]. `build_tunnel` pierces FEAT_WALL_OUTER, so corridors reach the `%` ring (generate.c:2678-2705). It avoids FEAT_PERM_OUTER and FEAT_PERM_SOLID. Because vaults have no secret door gap at the `%` ring, a corridor ending at a wall may be a vault approach.
8. Glyphs [SEEN]. terrain.txt: granite basic/inner/outer/solid (56-59) and permanent basic/inner/outer/solid (60-63) all use `G:#:w` (white `#`). The inner/outer/solid variants set `M:` to 56 and 60. The server applies `f_info[feat].mimic` when not in graphics mode (cave.c:921-923). The `VIEW_GRANITE_LITE` option (cave.c:931) only treats white features the same way. So a text-mode client sees a permanent wall as exactly the same as a granite one. The only tell is the shape and the failure to tunnel through (not tested). I did not read the tool client's own colour handling (c-tool.c) or pilot glyphs.py for walls. glyphs.py handles monsters only (grep showed no wall handling).
9. Vault-like things that are not vaults [SEEN]. These are the only other MAngband structures I found.
   - Wilderness buildings: `wilderness.c:1025-1139` builds houses, with moats (drawbridge `=`, water) on large buildings (area>=70, probability rising to 75% at >=130). 30% of large houses become PvP arenas (FEAT_PVP_ARENA, terrain.txt 0x5F "arena entrance", `#`, blue). These are surface (wilderness) features, not dungeon.
   - Town stores and homes use FEAT_PERM_EXTRA (generate.c:3440, 3720).
   - Dungeon-side confusables that look like walled blocks: monster pits (type 6) and nests (type 5), plus inner rooms. Pits and nests use `#` inner walls (FEAT_WALL_INNER) and are lit often. I did not read build_type3/4/5/6 beyond the call sites.
   - Dungeon boundary is FEAT_PERM_SOLID (3250-3283). That is a permanent wall, but at the map edge.
   - The `Lesser vault (arena)` is just a template name in vault.txt.

## Contradictions and surprises
- Against the premise that vaults have "permanent outer walls" (also HANDBOOK.md:265 "open vaults (permanent walls)"): the outline is granite `%`. Permanent `X` is only inside the vault. [SEEN]
- The brief's "lesser vaults at 250-1000 ft" is correct for the gate (>=250 ft) but the per-level odds are small. If the user saw several vaults in one mission, it suggests many levels or a different generator path; I could not check which.
- The vault.txt header says "maximum size 44x22" for lesser (the `### Simple Vaults (type 7) ###` heading, vault.txt:52; the header comment at line 17 says 33x22) but code and the parser cap is 33x22. Code wins.
- Within vault.txt there are 7 type-8 entries named "Lesser vault", so names are unreliable for the type. The CSV type column follows the X: line.
- Monster levels used for placement are `Depth`, in dungeon levels, not feet.
