# Dispatch: Borg history, failure modes, and cheats vs. screen-reading
- Brief: `/projectnb/jbrcs/mangband/Advisor/studies/2026-09-28-borg/briefs/D6.md` — Borg git history (299
  commits), documented weaknesses/tooling, cheat vs. screen-read split, and forum reports of what kills it.
- Clerk run: 2026-09-28; sources covered: `git log`/`git show` on
  `/projectnb/jbrcs/mangband/Advisor/data/borg/angband-src` (`src/borg`, 299 commits, 2011-2026),
  `docs/hacking/borg.rst`, `src/borg/borg.txt`, `src/borg/borg.c` header, `grep cheat` across
  `src/borg/*.c`, one angband.live forum thread.

## Answer
The Borg's git history shows the same handful of failure classes recurring for 15 years: getting
physically stuck (perm walls, lava, digging), oscillating/"bouncing" between levels or up-down the
same stairs, misjudging danger from stun/light/darkness-loving classes, drinking the wrong potion
under stress, and message-parsing gaps (missed death-message strings) that leave its internal model
out of sync with the game. Maintainers describe it as "fairly successful, having won the game several
times" (`borg.txt:7`) but never claim it is safe on a live save (`docs/hacking/borg.rst`: "not
recommended to run the Borg on a live game"). Its architecture is built around deliberately *not*
parsing the screen for inventory, equipment, spellbooks, monster HP, wall-permanence, and lighting —
it reads game structures directly ("cheat") because screen-parsing was judged too slow/flashy
(`src/borg/borg-think.c:159-171`). This is the opposite of our Pilot's screen-only design, so every
Borg mechanism that depends on a cheat needs a screen-derived substitute or an explicit "we can't know
this" fallback.

## Findings
1. **The Borg was removed from vanilla Angband entirely for ~9 years and rewritten from scratch.**
   `docs/hacking/borg.rst` (top of file): "It was pulled into the game in around 3.3. It was removed
   in 4.0 as there was too much conflict with the big changes made then. It was reincorporated in
   around 4.2.3." [SEEN] The git log confirms this exactly: commit `515ded1e` (2014-01-26) "Excise
   borg (thanks elly)" removes it, and the log is silent on `src/borg` from 2014 to 2023, then
   `80018afa` (2023-07-30) "Borg 4x (#5676)" and a wave of "Borgsource"/"Borg reorg" commits
   (`1665c15d`, `b2a79cb6`) reintroduce it. [SEEN] (`D6-gitlog.txt` lines 210-211 vs 182-185, scratch
   copy of `git log --oneline -- src/borg`)
2. **"Twitchy" loop-breaker: an explicit stuck-counter (`borg.times_twitch`) that escalates
   responses.** Declared `src/borg/borg.h:224` ("how often twitchy on this level"). It increments each
   time normal planning fails to find an action (`borg-think-dungeon.c:2124`, `:2247`), and gates
   increasingly drastic escapes: phase door tried while `times_twitch < 3` (`borg-think-dungeon.c:2126-2127`),
   full teleport while `< 5` (`:2249-2250`), digging is allowed to attempt even "hard" walls once
   `times_twitch > 21` by lowering `dig_check` (`borg-flow-misc.c:93` sets a wider leash;
   `borg-flow.c:135-136` reduces the dig-difficulty requirement), and once `times_twitch > 50` the
   escape code (`borg-escape.c:1240-1278`) forces a phase-door or teleport *purely because it is
   twitchy*, independent of the normal danger calculation, then resets the counter to 0. [SEEN] This
   is a generic "if my model of what to do keeps failing, treat that itself as danger" pattern.
3. **Fix: "got stuck beating head against perm wall."** Commit `04ef0c7a` (2024-10-09) adds to
   `borg_play_step()`: `if (ag->feat == FEAT_PERM) return false;` and the same for lava without fire
   immunity (`borg-flow.c:1004-1010` after the fix). Before the fix the Borg's step-taking code fell
   through to generic "tunnel or melt" handling for any wall-like feature and could repeatedly try to
   dig a permanent wall — a genuine unrecoverable loop, hit while "trying to dig out vaults" per the
   commit's own comment. [SEEN]
4. **Fix: "try digging out of stuck situations."** Commit `afbd360e` (2024-08-08),
   `borg_can_dig()` (`borg-flow.c` at the time; now `borg-flow.c:135-136`): once
   `borg.times_twitch > 10` the required dig skill (`dig_check`) is lowered by
   `MIN(times_twitch - 10, 19)`, i.e. the Borg becomes willing to try even unlikely digs the longer it
   is stuck. Direct evidence that stuckness is treated as an escalating override on normal
   preconditions, not just a one-shot fix. [SEEN]
5. **Fix: hallucinating + afraid could get the Borg stuck.** Commit `1491fa47` (2024-07-11): "add a special case for hallucinating and afraid to avoid getting stuck" — a status-combination (fear blocking melee, hallucination corrupting its view of surroundings) fell through normal logic with no valid action. [SEEN title/intent; diff not re-opened]
6. **Fix: potions-as-food logic could drink risky potions while starving without checking
   resistances.** Commit `df973d13` (2023-08-05), `borg_eat_food_any()` (then `src/borg/borg6.c:739`,
   now split into current files). Before: scanned *all* potions in inventory for nutrition value and
   would drink `sv_potion_sleep`/`slowness`/`blindness`/`confusion` whenever hungry, with **no check**
   for `BI_FRACT` (free action), `BI_RBLIND`, or `BI_RCONF`. After: only drinks those debuff-potions
   `if` the matching resistance/immunity trait is present; otherwise falls back to healing potions.
   [SEEN diff] This is a "wrong item used under stress" bug: fixing hunger by drinking Potion of
   Sleep while not immune to sleep is a self-inflicted death risk.
7. **Fix: stun-danger formula was over-broad, then wrongly excluded a friendly strategy.** Commit
   `b8e93eed` (2024-10-21), `borg_danger_physical()` in `src/borg/borg-danger.c`. Before: any attack
   `method->stun` added a flat `+400` "kick, tends to KO" danger bonus to *any* stunning attack.
   After: the bonus (`n += 400` at `borg-danger.c:121`, gated at line 120) applies only when
   `d_side < 3 && d_dice > 5` (a specific "many small dice" stun-attack shape, matching the commit's
   note that "10d2 is common but low sides and high dice count as dangerous"). The same commit
   removes a rule that blocked spell-bolt attacks and monster-chasing during "money scumming" in town
   (`borg-fight-attack.c`, `borg-flow-kill.c:1683-1686`), because with the old flat stun penalty
   town monsters looked artificially too dangerous to farm for gold. [SEEN] Shows how a coarse
   danger-formula bug can cascade into an unrelated feature (money-scumming for gold) being
   effectively disabled.
8. **Fix: necromancers ("like the dark") were treated as light-starved even when they could see
   fine.** Commit `d22eec1f` (2024-11-07) replaces checks on `borg.trait[BI_CURLITE]` (radius of
   carried light) with `borg.trait[BI_LIGHT]` (effective light radius, which can be nonzero for
   dark-loving casters with zero carried light) in `borg-caution.c:1144-1147`, `:1205-1206`, and the
   line-of-sight computation `borg_update_light()` in `borg-cave-light.c:87-146` (five separate
   `BI_CURLITE`→`BI_LIGHT`/`item.iqty` swaps). Before the fix a necromancer Borg would think it had no
   light and refuse to descend / panic for a light source it didn't actually need. [SEEN] Lesson:
   conflating "carrying a light item" with "can see" breaks for any character whose vision doesn't
   depend on a lit item — analogous risk for us if we ever hard-code "light source present" instead of
   what the screen/messages actually show about visibility.
9. **Fix: message-string matching for monster death was incomplete.** Commit `28baec67`
   (2024-10-31), `src/borg/borg-messages.c`: the `suffix_died[]` string-match table (used to detect a
   monster died, from the game's free-text combat messages) was missing many valid suffixes ("die.",
   "are destroyed.", "shrivel(s) away in the light!", "dissolve(s)!", "disintegrate(s)!", "freeze(s)
   and shatter(s)!", plural forms, etc.) — only `" dies."`, `" is destroyed."`, `" is drained dry!"`
   were matched before. [SEEN diff] Also fixed a related bug in the same commit: continued-message
   concatenation was dropping the first character of the continuation (`msg + 1` → `msg` at
   `borg-messages.c:1239`). **This is the single most relevant finding for us**: the Borg — despite
   having full internal game-state access elsewhere — still relies on free-text message *string
   matching* for kill detection, and that matching table needed years of patches to stay complete;
   our Pilot, which reads *only* the screen/messages, inherits exactly this fragility with no internal
   fallback to fall back on.
10. **Fix: level/stair "bouncing" — restock and go back down immediately, then bounce back up.**
    Two related fixes: `19944fc9` (2024-12-21) adds a grace timer before *fleeing* to restock:
    `&& (borg_t - borg_began) > 400` guarding the flee-to-restock branch in `borg-caution.c` (now
    `borg_timer(borg.time.level) > 400` at `borg-caution.c:1076`); and `2e1e1d16` (2025-01-10)
    reworks `borg_restock()`/`borg_prepared()` to remove a duplicated "ignore restock issues for the
    first N turns" special case that had been causing "bouncing depths" (the commit's own title), and
    adds `borg_must_return_to_town()` (`borg-prepared.c:828`) as a single source of truth. The current
    code still gates the *leaving*-for-restock decision on `borg_timer(borg.time.town) > 200`
    (`borg-caution.c:1063`). [SEEN] Direct evidence of an oscillation failure mode (arrive on a level,
    immediately decide to leave for supplies, come back, repeat) and the fix pattern: a minimum
    turns-on-level/turns-since-town timer before allowing a "leave" decision to fire.
11. **Cheat vs. screen-read — explicit design rationale.** `src/borg/borg-think.c:147-171` states the
    Borg has both screen-parsers and "cheat" functions for inventory/equipment/spellbooks, and
    deliberately uses the cheats: "These can be replaced by (very expensive) 'parse' functions, which
    cause an insane amount of 'screen flashing'... the 'cheat_inven()' and 'cheat_equip()' functions
    are expensive." [SEEN] So even the Borg's authors treat pure screen-parsing as the "honest but
    costly" path and chose to cheat instead — the opposite tradeoff from our Pilot, which cannot cheat
    at all (no game-internals access) and must accept the screen-parsing cost/fragility they avoided.
12. **Enumerated cheats found by `grep cheat`** (`src/borg/*.c`), each opened and confirmed:
    - Monster HP/percent-health: reads `m_ptr->hp`/`m_ptr->maxhp` directly from the live `struct
      monster` instead of the on-screen health bar (`borg-flow-kill.c:118-150`, own comment: "Borg
      does not look at the bar at the bottom... this cheat is not too bad. A human could draw the
      same info from the screen."). [SEEN]
    - Wall permanence: reads `cave` grid feature data to know if a wall is `FEAT_PERM` rather than
      inferring it from repeated failed digs (`borg-update.c:398-414`, own comment: "this is a humongo
      cheat... he is pulling the grid information from the game rather than from his memory"). [SEEN]
    - Item identity/flags: `borg_item_analyze()` "cheats all the information, and maybe is getting
      information that the player doesn't always get" (`borg-item-analyze.c:427-434`), though it does
      gate on `object_fully_known()`/`item->ident` to at least respect ID state. [SEEN]
    - Inventory/equipment/spellbook contents: `borg_cheat_spells()` (`borg-magic.c:635-714`) and the
      unnamed inventory/equipment "cheat_inven()/cheat_equip()" path described in finding 11, called
      every think-cycle from `borg-think.c:186-194`. [SEEN]
    - Quiver contents: `borg_cheat_quiver()` (`borg-inventory.c:132`, called `:208`). [SEEN name/call site only]
    - Chests: "The borg will cheat when it comes to chests" (`borg-flow.c:768`, comment only). [SEEN comment only]
    - Trap/glyph visibility is explicitly *not* a real cheat per the maintainers: "this checks that
      the trap is visible so it isn't really a cheat of any information the borg couldn't get another
      way" (`borg-update.c:329-330`). [SEEN]
13. **Tooling for post-mortem analysis:** `borg_cheat_death` config (`borg.txt:91-99`,
    `borg-init.c` diff in `69055a29`) flips the game's own `cheat_live` option so the Borg keeps
    playing after death for continuous testing; `borg_save_death`/`borg_dump_level` (`borg.txt:148-158`)
    control a character-dump + `borg-log.txt`/`borg.dat` write on death (`borg-log.c:50-56` gated on
    `BORG_SAVE_DEATH`, added in the same commit); the `l` command creates a `.map` snapshot with ASCII
    map, recent messages, full character state and internal trait/danger values
    (`docs/hacking/borg.rst`, "Log Snapshot" section). [SEEN]
14. **Forum report (angband.live), thread "Borg bugs and feature requests"**
    (https://angband.live/forums/forum/angband/vanilla/10687-borg-bugs-and-feature-requests): a NULL
    `k_ptr` segfault when launching a bolt attack, traced to `borg_takes` entries being cleared from
    the array while still referenced by the grid, and a separate segfault during resurrection from an
    uninitialized `known` flag on gear (both reported ~Aug 2023, matching the cluster of crash-fix
    commits dated 2023-08-07 to 2023-08-18 in the git log, e.g. `eeb5131b`, `80eb0630`, `64d6124e`).
    [REPORTED, dates corroborated against SEEN commit dates] Also reported: the Borg "spends
    excessive time on low-quality dungeon levels" and a poster (Estie) suggested a level-quality

## Failure-class table

| Failure class | Example commit/post | Fix | Lesson for us |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stuck / infinite loop (perm wall) | `04ef0c7a` "got stuck beating head against perm wall" | Reject `FEAT_PERM`/unfireproof-lava tiles as steppable before falling into generic dig logic | Any "try to path/dig through obstacle" logic needs an explicit unconditional reject list, not just a fallback that assumes all obstacles are diggable |
| Stuck / infinite loop (general) | `borg.times_twitch` mechanism (`borg.h:224`, `borg-think-dungeon.c`, `borg-escape.c:1240`) | Escalating counter: try phase (n<3), teleport (n<5), easier digging (n>10/21), forced escape regardless of danger (n>50), then reset | A generic "actions taken with no progress" counter that forces escalating fallback behavior, independent of the normal decision logic, is a cheap general-purpose loop-breaker we should have too |
| Wrong item used under stress | `df973d13` "rework how potions are used to counter starvation" | Only drink debuff-causing nutrition potions if the matching resistance trait is held; otherwise use plain healing potions | Any "use whatever is available to solve problem X" rule must gate on side-effect safety, not just the item's primary tag |
| Bad danger estimate (stun) | `b8e93eed` "change how the danger of stun is calculated to let money scum work better" | Flat "+400 if any stun attack" → conditional on specific dice/side shape (`d_side<3 && d_dice>5`) | Coarse binary danger flags can produce false positives that quietly disable unrelated strategies (here, town money-farming); prefer graded/conditional formulas over binary flags |
| Bad state read (light/vision) | `d22eec1f` "necromancers like the dark" | Use effective light radius `BI_LIGHT`, not carried-light `BI_CURLITE`, for "can I see" decisions | Don't conflate "carrying a light source" with "able to see"; base go/no-go decisions on the actual perceivable-state signal, not a proxy for it |
| Message parsing gap | `28baec67` "there are more death messages I missed" | Expand hardcoded death-message suffix table; fix message-continuation concatenation | Free-text message matching for game-state changes (e.g. "did my target die?") needs an exhaustive, actively-maintained string table; a screen/message-only bot (like our Pilot) has no cheat fallback when a phrase is missed |
| Loop / oscillation (levels & stairs) | `19944fc9` + `2e1e1d16` "hop up and down the same stairs" / "bouncing depths" | Add minimum turns-on-level (`>400`) and turns-since-town (`>200`) timers before a restock-driven "leave" decision is allowed to fire | Any auto-decision to backtrack (return to town / go back upstairs) needs a cooldown/hysteresis timer, or it will oscillate with itself each time it re-evaluates |
| Crash from stale references | forum: NULL `k_ptr` bolt-attack segfault; `a04bfefa`, `17cce240`, `944e6756`, `85004df1` (2011 crash fixes) | Null-check before use; guard array-clear vs. still-referenced entries | Recurring crash class across 13+ years: internal caches (kills/takes/items) outliving the game object they mirror. Relevant if our Pilot keeps its own map/monster cache — invalidate on the same event that invalidates the game's copy |

## Transfer notes
- **`times_twitch`-style stuck counter [INFERRED]:** directly portable — count consecutive
  Pilot decision-cycles where position/HP/inventory haven't changed as expected, and force an
  escalating fallback (retreat toward stairs, then stair-scum) once the count crosses thresholds,
  independent of the normal danger check. Real-time and multiplayer don't break this; if anything they
  make it more necessary since a human or monster could be the actual cause of the stall.
- **Restock/leave cooldown timers [INFERRED]:** directly portable — gate any "go back upstairs to
  restock/shop" decision on minimum turns-since-arrival, to avoid oscillation; in a shared,
  persistent-level, real-time game this also avoids repeatedly regenerating cost for other players
  (the level only resets when everyone leaves per `common.md`).
- **Message-string matching for kill/death detection [INFERRED]:** this is the part of the Borg
  closest to our Pilot's actual situation (no cheat fallback). Its 13-year history of "missed a
  message" bugs is a warning: budget for an ongoing, evidence-driven, versioned list of message
  strings (kill, death, resist, hunger, etc.) rather than a one-time table, and prefer message
  *patterns* over exact literal matches where the game's phrasing varies by monster name/plurality.
- **Danger-formula binary flags (stun, etc.) [INFERRED]:** avoid single boolean "this attack type is
  scary" flags feeding a flat danger bonus; the Borg had to walk this back once it produced false
  positives that broke a separate feature. Prefer thresholds tied to the actual attack parameters we
  can observe (damage dice/sides, or in our case, observed damage taken).
- **Light-vs-vision conflation [INFERRED]:** not directly relevant to our Warrior (no dark-vision
  class), but the general lesson — don't proxy a capability check with an item-possession check —
  applies to e.g. "do I have Free Action" vs. "am I actually immune to paralysis right now."
- **Cheats (monster HP, wall permanence, full inventory/equipment/spell state, item identity) [INFERRED]:**
  none of these are available to us; our Pilot must substitute screen-derived proxies (health-bar
  text/percentage if displayed, inferred wall-diggability from repeated failed attempts, message-based
  inventory tracking) and should expect the same "some information is just unavailable, act more
  conservatively" ceiling the Borg's own authors accepted for chest-traps and non-cheat cases.

## Contradictions and surprises
- Nothing here contradicts the brief's "already known" list directly. Most surprising fact: the Borg
  was **excised from the codebase for ~9 years** (2014-2023) and rewritten, not continuously maintained
  since 2011 as the git log's start date might suggest at a glance. [SEEN: `git log`, `docs/hacking/borg.rst`]
- The Borg's own architecture treats "cheating" as the *default*, efficient path and screen-parsing as
  the expensive fallback (`borg-think.c:147-171`) — the reverse of our situation, where screen-parsing
  is the *only* path available. Most individual Borg mechanisms (danger formulas, item selection)
  assume perfect, instant, structured state we won't have; only its control-flow patterns
  (stuck-counters, cooldown timers) transfer cleanly.
- Commit `b8e93eed` bundles a danger-formula bug fix with a feature-unlock under a loose causal note
  ("to let money scum work better") — a reminder to check whether a "bug fix" commit quietly also
  changes feature availability.
