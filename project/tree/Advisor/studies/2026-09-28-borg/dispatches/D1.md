# Dispatch: The Borg's danger model (`borg_danger` and friends)
- Brief: how borg-danger.c computes/aggregates/uses per-grid "danger", its memory of fear, and its known approximations
- Clerk run: 2026-09-28; sources covered: common.md; borg-danger.c (read in full, 2867 lines); borg-danger.h;
  borg-update.c (fear grids, avoidance reset, message-driven fear, 1 turn = 10 game turns table in
  game-world.c); borg-caution.c, borg-flow.c, borg-fight-attack.c, borg-fight-defend.c, borg-escape.c,
  borg-think-dungeon.c (grepped all `borg_danger(`/`borg_danger_one_kill(` call sites and read the
  surrounding threshold logic at each)

## Answer
`borg_danger()` sums, over every currently-tracked monster, `borg_danger_one_kill()` = max(physical
danger, spell danger), each itself built from per-blow/per-spell raw damage using **maximum dice
value, not average**, then scaled by an energy/speed "reach" factor and capped at 2000; it adds two
"fear" terms (a slow-decaying per-region memory of unlocated attacks, and a same-turn recompute of
crowd density), and the grand total is capped at 2000. The number is compared everywhere against
`borg.avoidance` (= current HP, escalated when the borg is "stuck"), and callers gate fight / flee /
rest / refuse-to-move / heal / escape at various fractions and multiples of that value (thresholds
below). One central and surprising fact: the AC-based hit-chance discount that the code visibly
computes is **dead in almost the entire codebase**, because `borg_danger()` force-sets
`full_damage = true` right before its main loop (line 2849), so nearly every caller gets the
AC-blind, worst-case number regardless of what it asked for.

## Findings

1. **Two-part per-monster danger, capped.** `borg_danger_one_kill()` computes `v1` (physical, via
   `borg_danger_physical()`) and `v2` (spell/ranged, via `borg_danger_spell()`), scales each for
   reach/energy, then returns `p = MAX(v1, v2)` capped at 2000. [SEEN] borg-danger.c:2795-2800.
   `borg_danger()` sums this over all live monsters (`for i in borg_kills[]`), adds two fear terms,
   and caps the grand total at 2000 too. [SEEN] borg-danger.c:2824-2865. So **danger from several
   monsters is a sum, not a max**, but the sum saturates at a hard ceiling of 2000.

2. **Physical damage uses the maximum possible roll, not an expected value.** For a plain hit,
   `z = d_dice * d_side` (borg-danger.c:112) — e.g. 1d10 contributes 10, not the 5.5 average. Each
   of a monster's `mon_blows_max` blow slots is summed into `n` (borg-danger.c:94-541), with the
   blow's *effect* (poison, elemental, drain-stat, etc.) applying resistance discounts and adding a
   flat "extra fear" bonus for scary effects (e.g. `MONBLOW_PARALYZE` always adds `+200` unless
   Free Action; `MONBLOW_DISENCHANT` adds `+500` unless resisted). [SEEN] borg-danger.c:129-511.

3. **AC / hit-chance is computed but effectively bypassed.** `borg_danger_physical()` does have a
   to-hit-vs-AC formula: `chance = 150 - (((ac*300)/4) + (level+power)*3)`, floored at 5%, and only
   applied `if (!full_damage)` (borg-danger.c:521-537). But `borg_danger()` — the function almost
   everything calls — sets `full_damage = true` unconditionally right before its monster loop
   (borg-danger.c:2849), **ignoring whatever the caller passed in**. Grepping all ~70 call sites of
   `borg_danger(` and `borg_danger_one_kill(` in `src/borg/*.c`, only two call
   `borg_danger_one_kill(..., false)` directly (borg-fight-attack.c:1306, 1328), used solely to
   decide whether it's worth waking a sleeping monster / to score ranged-attack target priority —
   not for the fight/flee/rest decisions. [SEEN] Everywhere else, physical danger is the AC-blind
   worst case. This directly qualifies the brief's assumption that danger folds in "hit chance vs
   AC" — it does, but only in a narrow side path.

4. **Reach/speed ("can it get to this grid this turn").** `q = c * ((t*e_monster)/10)` where
   `t = (100+(e_player-1))/e_player` is game-turns-per-player-action and `e_x = extract_energy[speed]`
   (table in `game-world.c:68`, normal speed 110 → 10 energy/turn). `c` is the caller's chosen
   "number of monster turns" to assume (see #9). For adjacent monsters (`d==1`), extra speed lets a
   monster hit more than once: `v1 = v1 * q/10` (borg-danger.c:2586-2588). For non-adjacent
   monsters, the code explicitly simulates one 8-direction move step, checks whether the resulting
   square is legal floor / whether a wall-passing monster is now adjacent, and only then lets the
   physical danger through, scaled by `q/(d*10)` (borg-danger.c:2523-2582) — this is the concrete
   "can it reach me this turn" check. Ranged/spell danger gets an analogous multi-step LOS
   simulation for fast monsters (borg-danger.c:2634-2690), with an explicit ASCII diagram
   explaining why a "currently safe" grid can be threatened next turn by a fast breather
   (borg-danger.c:2615-2633).

5. **Spells/breaths: hardcoded per-RSF formulas, many keyed to `hp/N` or `spell_power`.** E.g.
   `RSF_BR_ACID: z = hp/3 (max 1600)`; `RSF_BR_NETH: z = hp/6 (max 600)`; `RSF_BO_FIRE:
   z = 9*8 + spell_power/3`; summon spells (`RSF_S_KIN` … `RSF_S_UNIQUE`) do **not** evaluate the
   danger of what gets summoned — they use the summoner's own `spell_power` scaled by a small
   multiplier (×3/×6/×7/×12 depending on whether a glyph/PFE/unique is present) and divided by the
   count of free adjacent squares (`spot_safe`), with a flat 25% discount if the summoner is unique.
   [SEEN] borg-danger.c:619-2180 (full switch), summon pattern e.g. borg-danger.c:1466-1512.
   Each spell's "fear" bonus (`p`) is separate from raw damage (`z`); a bolt-type spell is zeroed
   if it can't project through walls to the grid (borg-danger.c:2183-2186). Frequency
   (`freq_innate`/`freq_spell`) only discounts danger `if (!full_damage)` (borg-danger.c:2774-2783)
   — again bypassed by `borg_danger()`'s forced `full_damage=true`, so **the primary path also
   ignores how rarely a monster actually casts its scariest spell.**

6. **Averaging vs "most dangerous spell."** `borg_danger_spell()` tracks both the average damage
   over all of a monster's known ranged attacks (`total_dam/ranged_attack`) and the single worst
   spell (`n`). If `average==false`, or `n >= av*1.5` or `n > CURHP*0.8`, it returns the worst-spell
   value `n`; otherwise the average. A long code comment explains this exists to avoid a specific
   bug: elemental-resistance-spell decisions could otherwise be starved by one un-resistible attack
   (e.g. Disenchant) dominating the average (borg-danger.c:2210-2266). [SEEN]

7. **Sleeping / status discounts, both physical and spell.** Asleep: halved if `CLEVEL>=25`, then
   *increased* again by the monster's own `r_ptr->sleep` alertness stat (light sleepers scarier)
   (borg-danger.c:2439-2450, 2712-2723). Confused: danger halved. Stunned: `*10/13`. Player-cast
   Sleep/Confuse spells on the monster divide its counted danger by `(d+2)` or force it to 0 if the
   borg is already below half HP and low level (borg-danger.c:2451-2510, 2725-2763). Unknown
   monster (`r_idx==0`) or "player ghost" (`r_idx >= r_max-1`): flat **1000** danger from both the
   physical and spell helpers (borg-danger.c:87-91, 599-603), and a separate flat **100** from
   `borg_danger_one_kill` for player-ghost indices specifically (borg-danger.c:2315-2317).

8. **Invisible/unseen attackers are not modeled per-monster; they raise regional memory instead.**
   When the borg is hit or missed or spell-hit by name that it can't locate on the map, it calls
   `borg_fear_regional(who, y, x, k, seen_guy=false)`: `HIT_BY` → `k = 4*(depth/5+1)`; `MISS_BY` →
   `k = 2*(depth/5+1)`; a parsed `SPELL_` message → `k = borg_fear_spell(...)`. This adds `k` (and
   smaller fractions to the 8 neighboring 11×11 regions) into `borg_fear_region[][]`
   (borg-update.c:2898-2916, 725-763). This region grid, not a per-monster estimate, is how
   "invisible monster" danger enters `borg_danger()` (term added at borg-danger.c:2835).

9. **The time horizon (`c`) is a knob, not a fixed constant.** Grepping every `borg_danger(`/
   `borg_danger_one_kill(` call: the overwhelming majority pass `c=1` (one monster-turn horizon;
   used for the default "am I in danger right now" checks throughout borg-caution.c,
   borg-flow.c, borg-fight-defend.c, borg-item-wear.c, borg-junk.c, borg-recover.c,
   borg-think-dungeon.c). `c=2` is used specifically for (a) evaluating a landing square before
   Phase Door / Teleport (`borg_caution_phase`/`borg_caution_teleport`, e.g. borg-escape.c:395,499;
   borg-fight-defend.c:2370) and (b) one melee-target-priority recheck
   (borg-fight-attack.c:324, 1048). `c=4` appears only in borg-fight-attack.c:3650-3757, in
   before/after comparisons for a specific multi-round combat-continuation decision. `c=turns` is a
   caller-supplied variable in the escape/teleport code (borg-escape.c:395,499,602). So "time
   horizon" is really "how many monster energy-turns do I assume it gets," reused ad hoc per
   caller, not a single global lookahead depth.

10. **`borg.avoidance` is the master threshold, and it is itself dynamic ("bravery").**
    `borg.avoidance = borg.trait[BI_CURHP]` on every new level (borg-update.c:2077,
    borg-think-dungeon.c:1376-1378) — i.e. **the danger tolerance scales with current HP**, not max
    HP. When the borg has nothing else to do (stuck/idle), it escalates in three stages, each
    tried and then reverted: ×2 CURHP → attempt anything → revert; ×4 MAXHP → attempt → revert;
    flat 30000 (plus resetting most memory: known stairs, glyphs, doors, uniques, breeders) →
    attempt → revert (borg-think-dungeon.c:2091-2244). [SEEN]

11. **Thresholds actually used to trigger behavior** (all against `p = borg_danger(...)`, most with
    `c=1`):
    - **Refuse to move into a grid ("icky"):** `danger > fear`, where `fear = avoidance*0.3`
      normally, `*0.5` at clevel 50, `*2` if `borg.mon.scary`, `*3` in a vault with a unique at
      clevel 50, `*5` if `borg.goal.ignoring`, `*25` if the level timer exceeds 5000 turns, `*100`
      if out of food (borg-flow.c:440-475). [SEEN]
    - **Flee the level ("excessive danger"):** `danger > CURHP*2` (borg-caution.c:1087), unless
      near a unique, at clevel 50, in a vault, or "ready for Morgoth."
    - **Take the stairs while already fleeing/leaving, or if standing on stairs and**
      `danger > avoidance` (or `> avoidance/2` below clevel 5) (borg-caution.c:1118-1121).
    - **Emergency confusion/blindness healing:** e.g. quaff Cure Serious/Healing if
      `danger < CURHP + heal_heal`; use a *Life/Star-Healing* potion instead if
      `danger >= CURHP*2` while `pct_down>=85`; refuse a risky teleport-staff fallback if
      `danger > avoidance*2` (borg-caution.c:145-207, 331-503).
    - **Escape ladders (Phase/Teleport/Staff):** dozens of `b_q > avoidance * X/10` checks with
      `X` from 3 up to 45 depending on which escape method and a `risky_boost` modifier
      (borg-escape.c:724-1178).
    - **Backup-swap gear / attack-spell gating:** `danger > avoidance/3` to swap gear
      (borg-caution.c:1027); `danger < avoidance*2` blocks a specific offensive spell
      (borg-fight-attack.c:2550).
    All of these read `borg_danger()`'s output as a plain number against a multiple of `avoidance`
    or `CURHP`/`MAXHP` — there is no separate "fear level" enum; fear is entirely numeric.

12. **`borg_fear_region` decays; `borg_fear_monsters` does not — it's rebuilt from scratch.**
    `borg_fear_region[][]` (11×11-tile regions) loses 1 point every 10 game turns
    (`if (!(borg.time.now % 10)) region--`, borg-update.c:2440-2450) and is fully wiped on
    entering a new level (borg-update.c:2139-2146). `borg_fear_monsters[][]` (per-grid) is zeroed
    **every** update tick and immediately recomputed from the currently-tracked monster list via
    `borg_fear_grid()`, which spreads `borg_danger(monster_pos)/10` outward in a diminishing radius
    (full at distance 1, `/2` at 2, `/3` at 3-4, `/5` at 5, `/8` at 6, via LOS) (borg-update.c:637-717,
    2453-2458, 2954-3023). So only the *region* fear is genuine memory; the *monster-crowd* fear is
    a same-turn recomputation, despite both being described as "fear" in the header comments.

13. **Approximations/oddities the code itself flags or that are visible on inspection:**
    - `borg_danger()`'s header comment calls the function "extremely expensive" and admits "we
      need to do more intelligent processing with the 'c' parameter, since ... the Borg does not
      realize that backing into a hallway is a good idea, since ... many of the nearby monsters can
      'squeeze' into a single grid" (borg-danger.c:2809-2817) — i.e. the model over-counts monsters
      that in the real dungeon topology couldn't all reach the grid.
    - Explicit design assumption: "we assume that monsters can walk through walls and other
      monsters to get to the player" (borg-danger.c:2276-2277) — an intentional worst-case
      simplification of pathing.
    - Dead code: in `MONBLOW_HURT`, `z = d_dice*d_side; if (d_side<3 && z > d_dice*d_side) n += 200;`
      (borg-danger.c:111-116) — the guard `z > d_dice*d_side` can never be true immediately after
      `z` was set equal to that same product, so this "stun" bonus never fires.
    - The AC/hit-chance formula (`borg_danger_physical`'s `chance`) has no upper clamp (only a 5%
      floor) and, plugged with typical values (power=60 for a plain hit, monster level in the
      single digits, any AC above ~0), saturates at the 5% floor almost immediately — so even where
      it *is* exercised (the two direct-call sites, #3 above) it behaves like a near-constant
      "assume a graze" rather than a real to-hit curve.

## Worked example: 4 adjacent orcs, 2×1d10 blows each, normal speed, vs AC 40 / 250 HP

**Assumptions marked [A]:** blow type = plain `MONBLOW_HURT` for both blows [A, "1d10" not
otherwise specified]; monster not asleep/confused/stunned, no PFE, no `BI_DAM_RED` [A, not stated];
player also normal speed 110 [A, only monster speed given]; `CURHP = MAXHP = 250` so
`avoidance = 250` on a fresh level [A]; monster r_idx known (not the "unknown monster" 1000-danger
case); no ranged attack (`v2=0`); pre-existing `borg_fear_region`/`borg_fear_monsters` = 0 (first
encounter) [A].

1. **Per-blow raw damage:** `z = 1*10 = 10` (max roll, borg-danger.c:112). Two blows → physical sum
   `n = 10 + 10 = 20`.
2. **Full-damage forcing:** because this danger is computed via `borg_danger()`, `full_damage=true`
   is forced (borg-danger.c:2849), so the AC=40 hit-chance discount (`chance` formula,
   borg-danger.c:524-537) is **not applied** — AC does not reduce this number in the path the Borg
   actually uses for fight/flee decisions. (For contrast, the bypassed formula would give
   `chance = 150 - ((40*300/4) + (level+60)*3)`, already deeply negative for any plausible orc
   level, clamped to the 5% floor — i.e. even in the path where AC matters, it would barely move
   the needle here; see Finding 13.)
3. **Reach/speed factor `q`:** normal speed both sides → `e=extract_energy[110]=10`,
   `t=(100+9)/10=10`, `q = c*(t*e/10) = c*10`. With the default `c=1`: `q=10`. Since `q` is not
   `>10`, none of the "extra attacks for a faster monster" scaling applies; `v1` stays 20.
4. **borg_danger_one_kill for one orc:** `v1=20`, `v2=0` (no spells) → `p = MAX(20,0) = 20`
   (borg-danger.c:2795-2800).
5. **borg_danger() sum over 4 orcs:** `p = 20+20+20+20 = 80` (plus the two fear terms, taken as 0
   here) (borg-danger.c:2852-2860). Far under the 2000 cap.
6. **Compare to thresholds** (avoidance = 250):
   - Movement-refusal bar ("icky" grid) ≈ `avoidance*0.3 = 75`: **80 > 75**, so the Borg would treat
     a not-yet-occupied grid adjacent to these 4 orcs as too dangerous to *voluntarily walk into*
     during pathfinding (borg-flow.c:446-475).
   - Level-flee bar = `CURHP*2 = 500`: 80 ≪ 500, no level flee (borg-caution.c:1087).
   - Stair-flee-while-on-stairs bar = `avoidance = 250`: 80 < 250, no.
   - Emergency-heal bars (`CURHP+heal_heal`, `CURHP*2`, etc.): all far above 80.
   **Net:** the Borg's own arithmetic calls 4 adjacent 1d10-orc-blows a minor threat (80/2000, 32%
   of current HP) that it would fight through rather than flee, but would *route around* rather
   than walk toward if it had a choice and were not already engaged — a genuine, code-visible
   distinction between "safe to keep fighting" and "not worth walking into."

## Contradictions and surprises
- Biggest surprise: `borg_danger()` silently overrides its own `full_damage` argument to `true`
  right before summing over monsters (borg-danger.c:2849). This means AC and spell/innate-attack
  *frequency* — both computed in the helper functions — are dead weight in nearly every call site;
  only two call sites elsewhere use the AC-aware path, and only for target-priority scoring, not
  for danger/flee decisions. This qualifies the brief's premise that melee danger folds in "hit
  chance vs AC" — the code has that machinery, but the primary aggregator doesn't use it.
- The raw per-blow damage is the maximum dice roll, not an expected value — "danger" here means
  worst-case single-hit damage summed across blows, discounted afterward mainly by
  resistance/effect logic and the reach/speed factor, not by rolling probability.
- `borg_fear_monsters[][]` (crowd/proximity fear) is described alongside `borg_fear_region[][]` as
  "fear," but only the region grid persists/decays; the per-grid monster-crowd fear is wiped and
  recomputed from scratch every turn, so it isn't memory at all.
- `borg.avoidance` — the main threshold the danger number is judged against — is itself dynamic: it
  equals current HP by default, but is escalated (×2, ×4 max HP, then a flat 30000) when the Borg is
  idle/stuck, each time reverting afterward. Any transfer of "the Borg's fight/flee threshold" needs
  to carry this escalator, not just a static fraction of HP.

## Transfer notes
- **Max-dice, not average, physical danger:** cheap and robust for a real-time bot too — precompute
  worst-case per-hit damage per monster type once (from `monster.txt`/an equivalent 1.5 table)
  instead of simulating dice at runtime. [INFERRED]
- **AC-blind primary path:** this is arguably a latent bug upstream, not a design to imitate; a
  MAngband danger table should keep the AC/to-hit discount live, since the Pilot has no other
  channel for "I have good armor, this monster barely threatens me." [INFERRED]
- **Energy/speed reach check (q, and the 8-direction "can it step and still hit me" simulation):**
  directly transferable in spirit — cheaper in MAngband since the server, not the bot, adjudicates
  whether a monster's turn lets it reach a square; the Pilot mainly needs "is monster N tiles away
  with speed S faster than me" as a coarse reach test, not the full simulation. [INFERRED]
- **`c` as a horizon knob (1 / 2 / 4 monster-turns) reused ad hoc per caller:** fragile and
  implicit even in Angband; for a from-scratch MAngband danger model, an explicit, named horizon
  (e.g. "this decision," "landing square," "one full combat round") documented per use is safer than
  copying the overloaded integer. [INFERRED]
- **Region-level "invisible attacker" memory that decays over ~10-turn ticks, wiped on level
  change:** directly analogous to what a stair-scumming, level-churning MAngband bot would want —
  but MAngband's persistent, multiplayer levels mean "wipe fear on level change" is wrong; fear of a
  region should probably persist per-level-instance and, since levels persist while anyone is on
  them, decay on a real-time clock rather than a game-turn counter. [INFERRED]
- **`avoidance` = current HP, escalating when stuck:** the *shape* (tolerance scales with your own
  current HP, and the bot gets braver when truly stuck) transfers, but the specific escalation
  triggers (idle-thinking loop with no other legal action) don't map onto a second-by-second,
  real-time Pilot, which is never "out of ideas" the way a batch-planning Borg can be. [INFERRED]
- **Summon-danger as a rough proxy of the summoner's own power, not the summoned monster's:** not
  worth copying literally; if MAngband's monster set has summoners, a from-scratch model should
  probably just use the historical/expected danger of what that monster is known to summon
  (per the danger table in `memos/2026-09-27-danger-table.md`), which the Borg's own code
  concedes it doesn't do. [INFERRED]
